## Choose Relevant Challenges

<!-- Prepare the important challenges the characters might face: combat, traps, significant skill
challenges, or other obstacles appropriate to the session. Include only the kinds the GM wants;
this is not a checklist requiring one of each.
Give each challenge a short title and a brief, evocative description of the situation. Explain
what the characters are trying to accomplish, what stands in their way, and the stakes, using
established facts or clearly marked proposals for GM approval.
Every challenge must have something meaningful at stake and the potential to affect the story.
State what could be gained, lost, protected, or changed, and how different outcomes could affect
what happens next. Consequences may change relationships, opportunities, information, resources,
or the situation itself; connect them to something the characters care about. Treat these as
possibilities, not predetermined outcomes, and leave room for bypassing the challenge or finding
an unexpected solution. If the stakes or story impact are unclear, ask the GM or propose them
for approval before treating the challenge as complete.
For combat, include relevant adversaries, equipment, and useful tactical or environmental details.
For traps, describe warning signs, triggers, effects, and possible ways to interact with them.
For skill challenges, describe the goal, pressures, possible approaches, and consequences.
Include mechanics when useful: relevant skills or checks, difficulty, how progress is tracked,
and what ends the challenge. Use the supplied rules reference where available. If the GM wants
custom mechanics, present them as a proposal for approval and distinguish them from established
system rules. Mechanics should support the stakes and allow different player approaches.
Keep the notes concise and usable during play; leave room for unexpected player solutions.
Use supplied rules references for statistics and mechanics. Leave unsupported mechanics open
unless the GM requests a custom proposal. -->
